03 / FROM THE THESIS

From roll calls
to structure.

Eigenpolitics uses the mathematics of networks to study the Senate. This explorer extends the original master’s thesis with reproducible data and an inspectable, evolving research companion.

Read the master’s thesis ↗
01

Encode the votes

Yea, paired yea, and announced yea become +1; corresponding nay positions become −1. Other recorded codes and missing entries become 0. Attendance and periods outside a member’s service therefore matter.

02

Construct the graph

Pearson correlation becomes a nonnegative weight, (r + 1) / 2. Remove self-loops and decompose the normalized graph Laplacian to reveal its spectral geometry.

03

Inspect the divisions

λ₂ summarizes connectivity. A Fiedler sweep estimates a low-conductance cut. Betweenness measures shortest-path brokerage in a thresholded network.

What these measures can—and cannot—tell us

These are descriptive properties of a constructed voting graph. They do not measure a senator’s influence directly, establish causation, or isolate polarization from the agenda and attendance. A Congress is a two-year cohort, so replacement members can appear alongside predecessors.

The eigenvector sign is arbitrary. Display orientation is made consistent by convention; coordinates across Congresses are not a shared ideological scale. Projection proximity is only a partial view of the full graph. Network ties show transformed correlation, not a percentage of votes in agreement.

The displayed sweep conductance is the best cut found along a sorted spectral coordinate, an upper-bound estimate of minimum conductance—not an exact Cheeger constant. The Gini coefficient describes the distribution of computed brokerage.

How this companion differs from the original scripts

The original thesis files and scripts are preserved in the repository. This version removes self-loops consistently, assigns edge distances on the graph actually used for betweenness, and avoids zero-length paths. The normalized spectral sweep uses a degree-adjusted coordinate. These corrections can change results relative to the thesis.

Methodology 1.1.0 excludes Marco Rubio from the 119th Congress because his very brief Senate service before becoming secretary of state provides only eight usable votes in the audited snapshot. This explicit analytical choice is applied before constructing the graph, so every coordinate and metric is recomputed. His earlier Senate records remain included.

Current data may also differ from the thesis’s data snapshot. Consult the repository’s methodology and provenance documentation before comparing exact values.
