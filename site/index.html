01

Encode the votes

Yea, paired yea, and announced yea become +1; corresponding nay positions become −1. Other recorded codes and missing entries become 0. Attendance and periods outside a member’s service therefore matter.

02

Construct the graph

Pearson correlation becomes a nonnegative weight, (r + 1) / 2. Remove self-loops and decompose the normalized graph Laplacian to reveal its spectral geometry.

03

Inspect the divisions

λ₂ summarizes connectivity. A Fiedler sweep estimates a low-conductance cut. Betweenness measures shortest-path brokerage in a thresholded network.

What these measures can—and cannot—tell us

These are descriptive properties of a constructed voting graph. They do not measure a senator’s influence directly, establish causation, or isolate polarization from the agenda and attendance. A Congress is a two-year cohort, so replacement members can appear alongside predecessors.

Aligned positions describe relative voting-network structure. The fit removes the shared senators’ collective translation and orientation, so it cannot measure a chamber-wide policy shift. Changing agendas, membership, missing votes, and eigenvector stability can still move points. Network ties show transformed correlation, not a percentage of votes in agreement.

The displayed sweep conductance is the best cut found along a sorted spectral coordinate, an upper-bound estimate of minimum conductance—not an exact Cheeger constant. The Gini coefficient describes the distribution of computed brokerage.

How shared-senator alignment works

The Geometry tab shows the original spectral coordinates and opens by default. The separate Aligned tab fits each two-dimensional spectral embedding to the preceding aligned Congress using senators present in both, starting with the first Congress. Rigid Procrustes alignment allows rotation, reflection, and translation, with no stretching or rescaling. This preserves within-Congress distances, eigenvalues, conductance, and brokerage. Network and Brokerage retain the original coordinates.

Shared senators are fitted as a group; they are not individually pinned. Their remaining displacement describes movement relative to that group. Selecting a senator keeps them tracked across the timeline. In Aligned, a hollow marker shows their preceding Congress’s position when both records belong to the same alignment segment. Click empty plot space to clear the selection.

Use the zoom buttons or scroll over the plot to inspect a cluster, then drag to pan. Each view keeps its viewport while you change Congress. Reset restores that view’s fixed global axes; comparison charts always show the global frame. Zoom changes the display only, not the coordinates or analysis.

At least three shared senators with a full-rank two-dimensional fit are required. A missing Congress or degenerate fit starts a new segment; links are never drawn across that break. Relative residuals above 0.5 are flagged as weak fits. Chaining many Congresses can accumulate alignment uncertainty. The aligned axes mix the two eigenvectors and are not separately the original Fiedler and third-eigenvector axes.

Method: orthogonal Procrustes, applied to centered shared-member coordinates, then translated to the previous frame. It uses only the thesis-derived spectral positions.
